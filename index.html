As LLM systems move toward long-horizon workflows involving tools, memory, and external evidence, I am interested in understanding when such agents can be considered reliable: whether apparent gains reflect meaningful capability improvements, whether they transfer across tasks and settings, and how agents behave when reasoning, memory, tool use, or feedback fails. Research Interests

  • Long-Horizon Evaluation and Failure Analysis I am interested in evaluating how failures in reasoning, memory, and tool use emerge on verifiable tasks and across new settings. I would like to use recurring failure patterns to guide targeted changes to agent systems, including their memory and adaptation mechanisms, and then test whether the resulting gains transfer to held-out tasks rather than remain specific to a particular benchmark. I am also interested in whether an agent’s confidence is calibrated to its empirical likelihood of success.
  • Agent Systems and Evaluation Harnesses I am interested in how planning, memory, tools, retrieved evidence, imperfect feedback, and execution control interact during extended workflows. I am particularly interested in whether system outputs are supported by observable execution trajectories, tool-use histories, and retrieved evidence, and how harness design influences both performance and failure behavior.
  • Controlled Environments and Interaction Data I am interested in exploring how controlled environments, interaction data, targeted failure scenarios, and verifiable feedback can support reproducible agent evaluation. Important questions include how representative these settings are, whether they expose rare but consequential failures, and how evaluation conclusions depend on benchmark construction, task design, and feedback quality. I would also like to test whether those conclusions remain valid across held-out tasks and settings.

Together, these directions connect controlled evaluation and failure diagnosis with targeted system improvements and validation on held-out tasks.

I am seeking research assistant opportunities to pursue these questions through rigorous empirical work. I can start immediately and commit substantial, consistent time for at least one year. I am inclined to relocate near the host institution to participate in on-site collaboration and support efficient communication. I plan to pursue a PhD.

Research Experience

EM and MCEM for Parameter Estimation in Regression Models B.S. Thesis · May 2025

A methodological study and R implementation of EM/MCEM estimators for Student's t regression and linear mixed models, combining derivations, simulation-based validation, and two real-data applications.

  • Derived the latent-variable posteriors for Student's t regression and linear mixed models, then implemented the resulting EM/MCEM algorithms in R. Used marginal GLS (ECME) updates for fixed effects, recomputed posterior quantities between variance-component updates, and kept MAP objectives for t regression and ML/REML objectives for mixed models explicit.
  • Added REML updates to reduce variance-component bias when the number of groups is small. Reproducible simulations with 200 and 500 replications assessed the correction; in matched single-dataset checks, deterministic EM agreed with optim, lme4, and nlme to within 10−8–10−6.
  • Applied the methods to facial-age modeling with 8,000 UTKFace embeddings and annotator response-time modeling with 59,699 CIFAR-10H trials from 299 annotators. Reworked the computations to avoid large dense matrices; the t model achieved a better in-sample fit than a Gaussian baseline, while the mixed-model implementation closely matched lme4.

CNN, BiLSTM & Transformer for Text Classification Independent · May 2025

A first-principles implementation and empirical comparison of CNN, BiLSTM, and Transformer text classifiers, relating architectural inductive biases and complexity to accuracy and inference throughput across four datasets.

  • Derived forward and backpropagation-through-time equations for vanilla RNN, standard LSTM, and peephole LSTM, and analyzed bidirectional recurrence, convolution, self-attention, positional encoding, and computational complexity.
  • Built a unified PyTorch benchmark across IMDB, AG News, BBC News, and SST-5 with GloVe-300d-initialized trainable embeddings and leakage-safe, train-only vocabulary construction.
  • Compared 12 model–dataset pairs under a shared protocol. CNN delivered the highest forward-pass throughput on all four datasets and the best or tied-best test accuracy on three; Transformer outpaced BiLSTM at 64 tokens but fell behind at 512, consistent with the expected sequence-length complexity trade-off.
